# Project Operating Manual

## Purpose

Project Fronkenschteen exists to develop a modular electric-guitar platform through disciplined, evidence-driven engineering. The engineering process is part of the project record, but it exists to serve the guitar program rather than become a competing product objective.

## Mission

Design and validate a distinctive, musically credible, manufacturable modular electric-guitar platform that combines proven luthiery, modern materials, serviceability, and artistic expression while maintaining rigorous engineering discipline.

Innovation shall be intentional rather than indiscriminate.

## Operating principles

### 1. Respect proven engineering

Project Fronkenschteen will not redesign established systems simply because it can. When mature luthiery practices satisfy project requirements, they are treated as engineering infrastructure rather than research projects. Conventional neck construction, proven adhesives and fasteners, established hardware, fretwork, setup practices, and appropriate traditional materials may be adopted without dedicated revalidation unless project evidence gives a specific reason to revisit them.

### 2. Innovate where it matters

Engineering effort will concentrate on areas that create meaningful project value or uncertainty, particularly composite wing architecture, wood/composite integration, wing and binding interfaces, art and material integration, internal structures, structural optimization, manufacturability, modularity, serviceability, and project-specific resonance behavior.

### 3. Evidence before confidence

Significant technical claims should identify the supporting evidence, assumptions, remaining uncertainty, and a validation plan when validation is appropriate. Validation effort is proportional to novelty, uncertainty, consequence, and project value; not every conventional design choice requires a new experiment.

### 4. Distinguish knowledge from belief

Project statements should be distinguishable as established fact, assumption, hypothesis, inference, or research question. This prevents assumptions from quietly becoming accepted truth.

### 5. Protect the innovation budget

Engineering time, money, prototypes, simulations, and attention are finite. The project prioritizes validation of meaningful uncertainty rather than validation of tradition. Established practices remain open to reconsideration if project evidence indicates they have become a limiting factor.

### 6. AI amplification

Artificial intelligence shall be used to amplify human creativity, engineering discipline, and decision quality—not to replace human authorship, responsibility, or vision.

Project Fronkenschteen treats AI as a multidisciplinary engineering collaborator rather than an autonomous designer. TJ, as Project Manager, establishes project vision, priorities, values, approvals, and final decisions. AI collaborators contribute analysis, organization, alternative perspectives, systems thinking, documentation, research support, and constructive critique.

The objective is not to automate creativity. The objective is to increase the reach, consistency, and effectiveness of human creativity.

## Human authorship and accountability

Project Fronkenschteen recognizes that ideas can emerge through conversation, critique, experimentation, and iteration. The project does not require every creative contribution to be artificially partitioned into isolated human or AI components. The human Project Manager remains the accountable author and decision-maker, with AI serving as an engineering collaborator that amplifies the human's ability to reason, explore, document, and execute.

Project success is therefore evaluated not by how much work AI performs, but by how effectively human judgment and agency are strengthened through collaboration.

## Engineering categories

Before allocating significant research or validation effort, subsystems and design questions should be classified where useful:

- **Category A — Established Practice:** adopt mature practice with minimal additional validation unless a project-specific issue arises.
- **Category B — Established Practice with Project-Specific Integration:** do not re-prove the underlying technology; verify that the implementation and interfaces work in the Fronkenschteen architecture.
- **Category C — Novel Engineering:** primary focus for analysis, simulation, prototyping, and validation.
- **Category D — Speculative Research:** potentially useful ideas that remain off the critical path until evidence justifies promotion.

## Roles and authority

### Project Manager — TJ

Responsible for project vision, priorities, approvals, scheduling, external relationships, business direction, and final project decisions.

### Chief Systems Engineer — Miles

Responsible for maintaining engineering coherence, traceability, systems integration, identifying missing evidence and technical risks, challenging unsupported assumptions, maintaining document consistency, and facilitating engineering reviews.

The Chief Systems Engineer advises. The Project Manager decides.

## Engineering workflow

Significant project changes follow a controlled path:

Idea → Discussion → Evidence Review → Candidate Decision → TJ Approval → Controlled Document Update → Git Commit → Baseline when formally approved.

Conversation is an important source of ideas and rationale, but conversation alone is not authoritative project knowledge. Controlled repository documents are the project record; approved baselines are the authoritative configuration.

## Decision discipline

A substantial decision should identify, in proportion to its importance:

- the problem or objective;
- rationale for the selected approach;
- meaningful alternatives considered;
- available evidence;
- important risks or consequences; and
- validation or verification needed, if any.

The requirement for a validation plan does not imply that every decision requires new testing. Established practice may be accepted on the strength of mature industry knowledge unless integration risk, project evidence, or consequence justifies additional validation.

## Engineering reviews

Formal reviews should periodically examine completed work, open questions, requirements status, risks, research progress, analysis and simulation status, action items, and the recommendation to proceed, revise, pause, or investigate further. Reviews exist to challenge ideas constructively rather than defend prior choices.

## AI consortium

The project may use specialized AI collaboration roles, including Chief Systems Engineer, materials, manufacturing, industrial design, acoustics/experimental analysis, research, business strategy, and IP/patent analysis. These roles are perspectives and analytical functions; they do not displace TJ's project authority or human accountability.

## Documentation hierarchy

Conversation generates and explores ideas. Controlled documents preserve project knowledge. Git preserves revision history. Approved baselines define authoritative project state.

When sources conflict, prefer the current approved baseline, then current controlled documents, then controlled engineering notebook/research records, then conversation history. Conflicts should be surfaced rather than silently reconciled.

## Project culture

Project Fronkenschteen values curiosity over certainty, evidence over unsupported opinion, iteration over premature perfection, transparency over ego, and disciplined restraint over novelty for novelty's sake.

The objective is not to prove ourselves correct. The objective is to discover what works, why it works, and whether it serves the instrument.

## Success

Success includes producing a musically useful, distinctive, manufacturable instrument and preserving enough engineering reasoning that another qualified person could understand, reproduce, critique, and extend the work.

A secondary research hypothesis may be tracked: disciplined human-AI collaboration may improve the agency of an individual inventor by increasing access to multidisciplinary analysis, documentation, and structured critique. This hypothesis is not assumed proven by the project and must not displace the primary objective of building guitars.
