# Validation Plan

## Principle

Every novel feature must be compared with the simplest credible control. Simulation informs decisions; physical testing validates the assembled system.

## Workstreams

### Tuning stability

- Define string gauge, tuning, environment, break-in, playing sequence, and measurement interval.
- Measure cents deviation by string after bends, chord work, vibrato, and time at rest.
- Diagnose tuners, stringing, nut friction, bridge, neck stiffness, and environmental effects as a system.

### Joint and structure

- Coupon tests for dovetail/attachment fit, insertion force, pull-out, torsion, creep, wear, temperature, and humidity.
- Tolerance-stack study across printing, machining, finishing, adhesive, and assembly.
- Failure-mode inspection and safe factor targets before full prototype.

### Materials and finish

- Density, stiffness, damping, print orientation, surface finish, adhesion, coating compatibility, UV exposure, and impact.
- Supplier and environmental documentation.

### Mass, balance, and ergonomics

- CAD mass properties followed by instrument measurement.
- Seated and standing player evaluation.

### Modal/acoustic behavior

- Compare simple ribs or uniform structures against Fibonacci, gyroid, honeycomb, or other candidates.
- Record mass and stiffness so acoustic comparisons are not confounded.
- Use repeatable excitation and instrumentation; avoid conclusions based solely on subjective listening.

### Player review

- Blind or partially blinded comparisons where feasible.
- Structured scoring for tuning, playability, balance, sustain perception, tone, finish, visual appeal, and overall confidence.

## Evidence ladder

Research → calculation → CAD/FEA → coupon → subassembly → Alpha instrument → Beta instrument → external player evaluation.
